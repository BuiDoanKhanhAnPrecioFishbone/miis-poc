1. What is to be delivered
1.1 The core of the assignment
A new, purpose-built web-based information system – MIIS – for the Mediation Office’s handling of information on mediation and collective agreements. The system shall support four core capabilities: registration/input, search, analysis and reporting of collective agreement data.
The system replaces today’s solution built as a customisation inside the W3D3 case management system (Formpipe), which has reached its limits: a capacity ceiling at about 3,500 agreements, reports that take unreasonable time or cannot run at all, a query builder limited to two document types simultaneously, and difficulties exporting data in a structured way. Data from both W3D3 and the earlier Access database shall be migrated to the new system.
Collective agreement monitoring covers central industry-level agreements where an employer organisation is a party. Local agreements, company agreements without an employer organisation, and application agreements (hängavtal) are out of scope and shall not be handled in MIIS.
1.2 Ownership and rights
The Mediation Office shall fully own the system with the right to freely use, maintain and further develop it without dependence on a single supplier. The authority shall have rights to source code, documentation, database structure, integrations and configurations.
1.3 Delivery strategy – two-stage rocket plus option
The system introduction is planned in two stages per Appendix 1, with a subsequent option (stage 3) per the call-off request:
Stage 1 (before 1 April 2027): Core functionality for registration, search and reporting of wage agreements and general terms, plus mediation – ready in time for the major bargaining round.
Stage 2 (autumn 2027 at the earliest): Remaining parts – pension agreements, other agreement types etc. Requirements marked "Stage 2" in the tables below belong here.
Stage 3 (option): A unilateral option for MI on a support and maintenance contract, 2 years extendable 2 + 2 years (max 6 years). Covers bug fixes, minor adaptations, technical support, security updates and some support, plus further development. Fixed annual fee for support/maintenance; variable hourly rates for development at the same rates as in the tender, indexed per Statistics Sweden’s AKI (SNI J), max 4% per year, earliest after 12 months. No guaranteed volume.
1.4 Requirement classification
Requirements in Appendix 1 are classified at three levels, reflected in the Class column of the requirement tables:
Mandatory (Ska-krav) – absolute requirements that must be met in the initial delivery. Included in the procurement.
Desirable (Bör-krav) – strong wishes, good to have but not decisive for approved delivery.
Option – wishes for future development. Not part of the initial procurement, but the system shall be designed to enable them.

4. Key concepts and data model
Appendix 1 (sections 4.2 and 4.5) defines the concepts at the system’s core. The requirements below refer to these:
Agreement (Avtal) – overarching entity with name and parties; one agreement is registered per party. Registration status Incomplete or Complete. Linked to the agreement: Wage agreements (with subgroups Minimum wage and Wage revision), General terms, plus Pension agreements, collectively agreed insurance and other agreements – each with its own validity period and signing date.
Agreement construction (Avtalskonstruktion) – seven constructions defined by MI describing how the wage scope is determined and distributed, incl. Local wage formation – figureless agreements and Wage pot without individual guarantee.
Agreement area (Avtalsområde) – groups several agreements with different parties.
Negotiation (Förhandling) – two types: Bargaining round (wages and general terms) and Other negotiation. Can be linked to an existing agreement or standalone with direct links to parties.
Negotiation procedure agreement (Förhandlingsordningsavtal) – agreements where the parties regulate mediation under their own procedure (currently nine). Decisive for whether MI appoints mediators.
Mediation (Medling) – decisions on mediation in union-level negotiations and in local disputes over collective agreements.
Party (Part) – types Employer organisation (AGO) and Employee organisation (ATO), with support for history and name changes.
Party meetings (Partsträffar) – meetings where MI meets one party ahead of a bargaining round. The parties do not meet each other; a party meeting is not a negotiation.
Report selections (Rapporturval) – types Eurofound, Minimum Wage, the MI website and the Short-Term Wage Report; properties on agreements governing report inclusion.
Cooperation body (Samverkansorgan) – an organised form of cooperation between unions; two types, Umbrella organisation and Cooperation, with the attribute Negotiating body Yes/No.
For agreement types that can be signed via a Party or a Cooperation body (Pension agreements, Other agreements), a link table to Agreement and a support table Signed by are constructed. The complete data model with support tables (Event log, Change log, Terms change, Watchwords) is in Appendix A of Appendix 1; the fields are examples and not exhaustive. Data is stored in a relational database, suggested Microsoft SQL Server.

5. System components and target architecture (on-prem)
5.1 System components
The system sketch in Appendix 1 (section 4.3) describes MIIS as three main parts: web application, Windows service and data layer. The components below follow the sketch, with requirement classification per component:
Web application miis.mi.se – the authenticated entrance for internal users, with the functional modules Agreement registration (wage agreements and general terms), Party management (AGO/ATO with history), Document management, Search & Reports, AI-assisted registration (OCR and field proposals), Change log (audit trail and snapshots) and Mediation management (mediators and party meetings). All are Stage 1 mandatory requirements.
Public entrance miis.mi.se/allmanheten – access for the Public computer via IP restriction on MI’s network, no login.
Windows service for scheduled report extracts – desirable requirement (FR-014).
Agreement registration for pension agreements, insurance and other agreements – Stage 2 mandatory, as its own module in the sketch.
Relational database – scalable data layer carrying the whole system.
Entrances and identity – Internal users (various roles) via the EFOS IdP at Försäkringskassan; External access for mediators via the BankID IdP at Försäkringskassan (option, Stage 2); the Public computer via IP restriction.
File exports – three outbound flows: Documents (Word), Reports (Excel, Word, PDF) and Data extracts (CSV, JSON – desirable).
5.2 Target architecture on-prem
The architecture figure below (Swedish labels) details the target architecture based on the premise that the entire solution is operated on-prem in MI’s server environment at Försäkringskassan (Appendix 1 section 4.6), without direct or indirect dependencies on external cloud services (NFA-001). The AI needs are met via Försäkringskassan’s Model as a Service within the same environment, identity via Försäkringskassan’s IdP (SAML 2.0/EFOS, Bank-ID in Stage 2) and permissions via the existing Enterprise IAM/SSID. Requirement references in the figure point to the corresponding Features in chapters 6–7. Layers top to bottom: Klienter = Clients, Åtkomst och säkerhet = Access and security, Applikationslager = Application layer, Datalager = Data layer, Migreringskällor = Migration sources, Filexporter = File exports.

The division of responsibility follows Appendix 1: the supplier is responsible for the application including application-level operations, backup of application data/configuration and RTO max 8 hours for application faults (NFT-002/003), while Försäkringskassan is responsible for the underlying infrastructure and server environment with an availability target of at least 99% of office hours provided the supplied environment is operational (NFT-001).

5.3 Technical solution architecture (supplier’s proposal)
While section 5.2 describes the logical target architecture, the figure below shows the proposed technical solution architecture with concrete components and technology choices. Appendix 1 prescribes no technology stack beyond a web-based client, a relational database (suggested Microsoft SQL Server) and on-prem operation; the following is therefore the supplier’s proposal, motivated against the requirements and adapted to the Windows-based operating environment at Försäkringskassan.
In brief: a React/TypeScript web client (SPA) with a restricted public mode, an API and service layer in ASP.NET Core (.NET 8 LTS) on Windows Server/IIS with domain modules per functional area, a .NET Worker Service running as a Windows service for scheduled extracts, reminders, retention and migration jobs, Microsoft SQL Server with temporal tables for version history/snapshots and full-text indexing for document search, file storage for documents, and integration adapters towards Försäkringskassan’s IdP (SAML 2.0), Enterprise IAM/SSID, Model as a Service (REST) and SMTP relay. Four environments are established in MI’s server environment: development, test, UAT and production.

8. Role-based user scenarios
The scenarios below (US-01–US-20) cover all eight user roles in Appendix 1 section 3.1 and all functional Epics in chapter 6. Each scenario is detailed at field level based on the requirement texts, the concepts in section 4.2, the AI support in section 4.1, the data model in Appendix A and the report examples in Appendix F, and is complemented with alternative and exception flows where the requirements indicate them. US-01 follows the user flow for agreement protocol registration that MI itself describes in Appendix 1 section 4.4.
Each scenario states which Features (chapters 6–7) it verifies, making the scenarios directly usable as acceptance test cases (T-005–T-008) with representatives of all user roles. The scenarios also form the backbone of our response to the award criterion Role-based user scenarios and user interface, and are complemented in the tender response with UI sketches per scenario.
US-01: Register an incoming agreement protocol with AI support
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
A new agreement protocol for an existing agreement is registered quickly and correctly with AI assistance at every step, and the protocol is linked to the agreement.
Preconditions
An agreement protocol has been received. The agreement already exists in MIIS. The case officer is logged in via EFOS with a role-adapted start page.
Verifies requirements
FAI-001–004, FA-001–004, FA-007–012, FA-018, FA-021, FD-001, FF-002, FH-001, FH-002, FR-012, FS-001, Appendix 1 §4.1 and §4.4
Main flow:
The case officer uploads the agreement protocol from the start page. Scanned documents are automatically OCR-interpreted (FAI-003) and the document is linked to the relevant entity (FD-001).
The system highlights text in the protocol based on the predefined and customisable watchword table, giving a quick rough overview of specifically selected demands – for example those identified at party meetings (FAI-004, §4.1).
AI analysis 1 – identification: the system looks for agreement area, agreement name and parties in the protocol and proposes which existing agreement it concerns, and that e.g. a new wage agreement or general terms agreement should be created (§4.1 Quick registration).
The system shows the pre-filled form "Register new/update agreement" with agreement name, alternative name, parties and agreement type (FA-001). Validation and logic checks help the officer avoid errors (§4.1). The officer adjusts as needed and approves manually (FAI-002).
The agreement exists in MIIS – AI analysis 2 proposes signing date, validity period and any termination option based on the protocol content (FAI-001).
The officer registers the wage agreement as a new row for the bargaining round (FA-002): the agreement construction is chosen among the seven MI-defined constructions, e.g. Local wage formation – figureless agreements or Wage pot without individual guarantee (FA-007, §4.2), wage increase scope and cost frame are entered (FA-008), individual guarantees (FA-010) and any working time reduction with cost (FA-009). The subgroups Wage revision and Minimum wage are handled linked to the wage agreement (§4.2).
Via free-text search in the protocol the system identifies and proposes registration of specific wording, e.g. on gender equality or working time reduction (§4.1). The officer approves and sets the gender equality flag where applicable (FA-011). If the agreement is part of the norm-setting industry agreements, the industry benchmark flag is set (FA-012).
General terms are registered with their own validity period and signing date – the periods need not coincide (FA-003, FA-004, §4.2).
If a negotiation is registered, it is linked to the newly signed agreement via the protocol upload (FF-002).
The registration is saved with status Complete. The change log records what changed, by whom and when (FH-001), the event log receives "agreement signed" (FH-002), and the agreement is colour-coded green as newly signed without mediation (FR-012).
Alternative and exception flows:
Agreement name not stated in the protocol: the system uses the file name or lists the parties’ joint agreements as identification input (FA-018, §4.1).
Information missing: the agreement is saved with registration status Incomplete and completed later, see US-04 (FA-021).
The agreement was signed after mediation: colour-coded red instead of green (FR-012), see US-09.
The AI proposal is wrong: the officer corrects freely before approval – nothing is saved automatically (FAI-002).

US-02: Register an entirely new agreement manually
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
An agreement never previously signed is registered fully manually, in line with the principle that entirely new agreements are always registered without AI automation.
Preconditions
A protocol for an entirely new agreement (no previous version in the system) has been received – one of the edge cases test data must cover (T-003).
Verifies requirements
FA-001–004, FA-007, FA-021, FD-001, FH-001, T-003, Appendix 1 §4.1 (Quick registration) and §4.2 (Report selections)
Main flow:
The officer uploads the protocol; the AI analysis finds no match against existing agreements and the system leads to the "Register new agreement" form (§4.4).
The officer enters agreement area (which groups agreements with different parties, §4.2), agreement name, alternative name, parties and agreement type manually (FA-001). Since MI registers one agreement per party, one registration is created per agreement party (§4.2).
Wage agreement is registered with construction, wage increase scope and validity period; general terms with their own period (FA-002–004, FA-007).
Report selection properties are set on the agreement – whether it should be included in Eurofound, Minimum Wage, the MI website and the Short-Term Wage Report respectively (§4.2).
The protocol is linked to the agreement (FD-001) and the registration is logged (FH-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
Information missing at registration: the agreement is saved with status Incomplete (FA-021) and followed up per US-04.

US-03: Handle an organisational change of a party
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
A name change or merger of a party is made in one place and propagates to all current agreements – without affecting historical agreements and without duplicate manual work.
Preconditions
An organisational change has been announced, e.g. of the kind where Lärarförbundet and Lärarnas Riksförbund formed Sveriges Lärare, or KFO and Idea merged into Fremia (§2.3). The party exists in the party register.
Verifies requirements
FP-001–006, FH-001, Appendix 1 §2.3 and §4.1
Main flow:
The officer finds the party in the party register using relevant properties as filters (FP-005).
The name change is registered with a validity date; the history of previous names is preserved – for AGOs with links to sector (private/state/municipalities and regions), employer group and industry code for organisations within Svenskt Näringsliv (FP-001), for ATOs with history of name changes and organisational changes (FP-002).
The system automatically propagates the new name to all current agreements, while historical agreements keep the name valid at their time (FP-004) – no affected parts need manual updates (§4.1).
Where needed, Cooperation body links are updated with time period, including type (Umbrella organisation or Cooperation) and the Negotiating body Yes/No attribute (FP-003, §4.2).
Contact persons (name, title, phone, e-mail) are reviewed for the party (FP-006). The change is logged with old and new values (FH-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
The party changes umbrella organisation, or negotiation rights move between unions (cf. Transportarbetarförbundet/Unionen §2.3): the links are updated with time periods so that history is preserved (FP-002, FP-003).

US-04: Incomplete registration, reminder and the Short-Term Wage Report
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
A partially registered agreement is followed up via a reminder and appears correctly in the Short-Term Wage Report view with status and protocol link, until registration is complete.
Preconditions
An agreement was saved with registration status Incomplete pending supplementary information. The agreement has the Short-Term Wage Report selection property.
Verifies requirements
FA-021, FA-022, FE-001–003, FR-008, FH-001
Main flow:
The officer sets a reminder on the agreement to update it on a given date (FA-022).
On that date, the system sends a reminder e-mail to the agreement administrator (FE-001). The e-mail contains a link that opens the agreement directly in the system (FE-002), and the sending is logged in the event log (FE-003).
The Short-Term Wage Report view lists the monitored agreements with a status column showing whether each agreement is registered or partially registered, plus a link to the protocol even when registration is incomplete (FR-008).
The view also shows which agreements have previously been exported to the report (FR-008).
The officer completes the registration, sets status Complete (FA-021) and exports/prints the report from the view. Changes are logged (FH-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
Supplementary information is delayed: a new reminder with a later date can be set (FA-022).

US-05: Confidentiality-mark an agreement
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
An agreement with sensitive information is confidentiality-marked so that details are hidden from unauthorised users and in public reports, while the agreement is still counted in statistics.
Preconditions
An agreement with sensitive attachments has been registered.
Verifies requirements
D-001, D-002, NFÅ-003, NFÅ-004, FR-011, FH-001
Main flow:
The agreement administrator sets a confidentiality mark on the agreement – only this role can set it, and it applies primarily to attached documents (D-001).
The system then hides the detailed information of the marked agreement from unauthorised users (mediators, the public) and in public reports (D-002, NFÅ-004).
When releasing protocols and agreement prints to mediators and the public, confidentiality-marked information is excluded (FR-011).
The agreement is still included in statistics (D-002). The marking is logged in the change log (FH-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
The confidentiality need ceases: the marking is removed by the agreement administrator and the change is logged (D-001, FH-001).

US-07: Create a mediation case from a GD decision with AI support
Role
Mediation administrator
Goal
An incoming Director-General mediation decision becomes a complete mediation case (Special mediation) with links to affected agreements and full decision support.
Preconditions
A GD mediation decision has been taken in a union-level negotiation. The decision appoints mediators (cf. the examples in Appendix E).
Verifies requirements
FF-006–008, FSD-001, FD-001, FA-017, FE-001–003, FR-012, FH-002, Appendix 1 §4.1 (Mediation cases)
Main flow:
The mediation administrator uploads the GD decision (FD-001); the system creates a mediation case of type Special mediation linked to the affected agreements (§4.1, FF-006).
The system lists the other parties in the agreement area and shows information about previous mediations and any contagion risks (§4.1).
The system shows whether the affected agreements are covered by a negotiation procedure agreement – decisive for whether MI appoints mediators (FF-006, FA-017, §4.2).
The mediation case is linked to several agreements where needed (FF-008), and the registry number from MI’s registry system is entered in the dedicated field (FF-007).
Where needed, the GD decision document is created from a template – the variant with or without industrial action notice – pre-filled with MIIS information that can be edited before finalisation (FSD-001).
When the mediation decision is finalised, a notification e-mail is sent to the mediator administrator (FE-001) with a link to the case (FE-002); the sending is logged (FE-003).
The event log receives "mediation starts" on the affected agreements (FH-002), and the agreements are colour-coded red as mediation-linked (FR-012).
Alternative and exception flows:
Local dispute over a collective agreement (Standing mediation): registered in simplified form in the separate form instead (FF-006), see US-09.
The agreement is covered by a negotiation procedure agreement: the parties mediate under their own procedure without MI involvement – registered per FA-017, and MI appoints no mediators (§4.2).

US-08: Register a party meeting and coordinated bargaining demands
Role
Mediation administrator
Goal
A party meeting is documented before, during and after the meeting, and coordinated bargaining demands are registered with links to the unions backing them.
Preconditions
A party meeting is booked ahead of a bargaining round – a meeting where MI meets one of the labour market parties to gauge the negotiation situation, identify conflict risks and assess the need for mediation. The parties do not meet each other, and a party meeting is not a negotiation (§4.2).
Verifies requirements
FF-004, FF-005, FSD-002, FD-001, FAI-004, FH-001
Main flow:
Ahead of the meeting, the mediation administrator registers preparatory information (FF-004) and creates a party meeting document from a template pre-filled with MIIS information (FSD-002).
During the meeting, information is entered directly in an interactive view (FF-004).
After the meeting, the registration is completed, the party meeting information can be printed, and the documentation is uploaded and linked to the meeting (FF-004, FD-001).
Coordinated bargaining demands are registered with a coordinated/own-union flag and linked to the unions backing the demand – e.g. the LO unions’ coordinated demands (§2.3). Documents linked to the demands are stored in the system (FF-005).
Demands identified at the meeting can be added to the customisable watchword table, so they are highlighted in future incoming protocols (FAI-004, §4.1).
Alternative and exception flows:
Information is supplemented afterwards: the registration can be updated both before and after the meeting, with full traceability (FF-004, FH-001).

Reports are generated within reasonable time regardless of data volume – no reports "hang" (NFP-002, cf. §2.5).
Recurring extracts are scheduled via the system’s scheduled report extract function (FR-014, desirable; the Windows service in the system sketch §4.3).
Alternative and exception flows:
A historical report state is desired: the report is produced against a snapshot reconstructing data as of a specific date (FH-003).
In Stage 2, reports for the MI website and for Eurofound and Minimum Wage are added, driven by the agreements’ report selection properties (FR-009, FR-010, §4.2).

US-18: Register supplementary agreement information
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
The agreement’s supplementary parts – minimum wages, working groups, insurance information, other agreements and mediation under the parties’ own procedure – are registered in a structured way.
Preconditions
An agreement is registered per US-01/US-02 and the protocol contains supplementary settlements.
Verifies requirements
FA-005, FA-006, FA-013, FA-014, FA-017, FH-001
Main flow:
Minimum wages are registered grouped by occupational group with revision date (FA-013; cf. the Minimum wage table in Appendix A with minimum wage in SEK, occupational group and revision date).
Working groups set up by the parties are registered with their subject areas (FA-014).
Insurance information is registered on the agreement (FA-005).
Other agreements are registered, e.g. negotiation procedure agreements – linked to specific central collective agreements but not necessarily to all of a party’s agreements (FA-006, §4.2).
Mediation under the parties’ own procedure via negotiation procedure agreements is registered (FA-017), which then drives what the mediation views show (FF-006, US-07).
All registrations are logged (FH-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
The settlement is signed at umbrella organisation level (e.g. by LO or PTK, §2.3): handled as pension/other agreements signed via Party or Cooperation body per US-20 (Stage 2).

US-19: Handle expiring agreements and early termination
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
Agreements that end – as planned or by early termination – are registered correctly so that the agreement picture and the reports reflect reality.
Preconditions
An agreement is approaching its expiry date, or a party has terminated the agreement early. Good knowledge of expiry dates is central to anticipating the scope of the bargaining round (§2.4).
Verifies requirements
FA-015, FA-016, FA-022, FE-001, FH-002, FR-006
Main flow:
The officer registers on the agreement that it expires and will not be renewed (FA-015).
For early termination, the termination is registered on the agreement (FA-016; cf. the early termination field on wage agreements in Appendix A).
The event is logged in the event log (FH-002).
The agreement is reported correctly in the Bargaining Round Report’s section on agreements expiring during the year (FR-006; cf. Appendix F report 3, "Agreements expiring during 2026").
Where needed, a reminder is set for follow-up of the agreement status on a given date, with an e-mail notification to the officer (FA-022, FE-001).
Alternative and exception flows:
The agreement is replaced by a new agreement with a different party constellation: the new agreement is registered per US-02 and the parties’ history is preserved (FP-001, FP-002).

US-20: Register pension agreements and full insurance information (Stage 2)
Role
Agreement administrator / Case officer
Goal
Pension agreements and full insurance information are registered, including agreements signed at umbrella organisation level, and the agreement picture can be displayed per point in time.
Preconditions
Stage 2 is live. Pensions and insurance are handled at umbrella organisation level – only LO and PTK sign their own collective agreements on e.g. pensions (§2.3).
Verifies requirements
FA-023–025, FH-001, Appendix 1 §4.5 (link tables and Signed by), NFM-002
Main flow:
The officer registers the pension agreement with up to three pension systems, each with wage thresholds and premiums (FA-023; cf. Appendix A: pension system name, wage threshold in SEK, premium in per cent, central or local).
The agreement is linked via a link table to the affected agreements, and the Signed by support table indicates whether it was signed by a Party or a Cooperation body (§4.5).
Full insurance information is registered with source and the categories illness, work injury, parenthood, death, transition and other (FA-024).
The officer can then display an agreement area and agreement with associated wage agreements, general terms and other agreements valid at a given point in time (FA-025).
The pension and other agreements report is produced with selections on parties for wage agreements/general terms and parties on pension and other agreements respectively (cf. Appendix F report 6).
Alternative and exception flows:
Historical pension agreement information from the Access database: migrated in Stage 2 with verified data quality (NFM-002, NFM-003) and becomes searchable together with newly registered information.

9. UI sketches per scenario
The sketches below (Swedish UI labels – Swedish is the language of the target system) concretise the prioritised user scenarios in a coherent MIIS interface. Each sketch is annotated with the Feature IDs (chapters 6–7) that the respective interface element realises, giving direct traceability from requirement to interface. The sketches are deliberately technology-neutral design input – final design is detailed together with MI’s users during the project, with WCAG 2.1 AA as the accessibility frame (NFUI-003).
9.1 Role-adapted start page (FS-001)
The start page for an agreement administrator: benchmark banner (FM-003), own reminders ("Mina påminnelser", FA-022), incomplete registrations ("Ofullständiga registreringar", FA-021), recently registered agreements with green/red/blue colour coding (FR-012) and the latest events from the event log ("Senaste händelser", FH-002). A mediation administrator instead sees ongoing mediations, per the requirement’s example.

9.2 US-01 – Register an agreement protocol with AI support
The registration view follows the steps of MI’s user flow (Appendix 1 §4.4), with the protocol on the left – OCR-interpreted (FAI-003) and with watchwords highlighted (FAI-004) – and the AI pre-filled forms on the right. Every AI proposal is clearly labelled ("AI-FÖRSLAG") and requires manual approval before anything is saved (FAI-002). The wage agreement section holds agreement construction (FA-007), wage scope/cost frame (FA-008), individual guarantee (FA-010), working time reduction (FA-009) and the gender equality flag (FA-011), with a separate validity period for general terms (FA-004) and the option to save as incomplete ("Spara som ofullständig", FA-021).

9.3 US-07 – Mediation case from a GD decision
The mediation case view gathers all decision support: the registry number field (FF-007), the negotiation procedure agreement indicator that determines whether MI appoints mediators (FF-006), several linked agreements with red colour coding (FF-008, FR-012), the AI panel with other parties, previous mediations and contagion risks (§4.1), mediators from the register (FF-009), the benchmark as reference in the mediator view (FM-003), document generation with and without industrial action notice (FSD-001), finalisation with notification (FE-001) and the mediation outcome fields (FF-010).

9.4 US-11 – Query builder with snapshot and export
The query builder without today’s limitations: choice of information type, selection criteria freely combinable with and/or, selectable presentation columns and saved searches (FR-002), free-text search in documents (FR-003), snapshot per any date (FH-003), response time within 3 seconds (NFP-003) and export to Excel and CSV/JSON respectively (FR-004, FR-013).

10. Requirements summary
In total, the requirements specification comprises 25 Epics: 11 functional (F1–F11) and 14 non-functional (NF1–NF14). Distribution of Features per class:
Functional requirements: 72 Features, of which 70 mandatory and 2 desirable (FR-013, FR-014). Five mandatory requirements are marked Stage 2: FA-023, FA-024, FA-025, FR-009 and FR-010.
Non-functional requirements: 59 Features, of which 58 mandatory and 1 option (NFÅ-007, Bank-ID for mediators, Stage 2). Among the mandatory requirements, migration of the Access database (NFM-002) is marked Stage 2.
All mandatory requirements must be accepted for the tender response to be evaluated, and all mandatory requirements are verified and approved by the Mediation Office before final delivery (T-006).
Chapter 8 contains 20 detailed role-based user scenarios (US-01–US-20) with field-level main flows plus alternative and exception flows. The scenarios cover all eight user roles and all functional Epics – including the external mediator access option (US-15) and the Stage 2 pension agreement functionality (US-20) – with traceability from scenario to Feature ID. Chapter 9 contains requirement-annotated UI sketches for the role-adapted start page and scenarios US-01, US-07 and US-11.
